# Intro film: "One line, one room"  (style: One-line Drawing)

Purpose: the 9-second opening of the course site. It plays once, muted (browsers only autoplay
silent video), and hands over to the hero image of Shahar, Claude and the butterfly.

## Three candidate structures
1. **Line as graph.** A learning curve runs right-to-left, flat, then climbs steeply into "level up".
   Clear message, but abstract, and the hero image that follows has nothing to do with a chart.
2. **Word to room.** "Claude" written in script; its last letter becomes the room.
   Latin script in a Hebrew right-to-left site reads backwards against the page; rejected.
3. **Room, then butterfly (chosen).** The pen enters mid-line at speed from the right edge
   (Hebrew reading direction), draws the loft studio of the course art in one continuous line:
   window grid, skyline, two pendant lamps, shelves, desk, chair. The last stroke slows and curls
   into the one-line butterfly of the Lnet mark, the only accent colour (butterfly purple, used once).
   The camera pulls back: the whole room was one picture, laid out exactly like the hero art,
   so the site's cross-fade to the full-colour image reads as the drawing coming alive.

## Logline and arc
A single line builds the studio the course happens in, and turns into the butterfly that
signs it. Setup (pen at speed, room appears) → turn (the line slows, colour arrives, butterfly) →
ending (pull-back reveal; the site replaces the drawing with the living picture).

## Benchmark
- Learn: Saul Bass-era title sequences (one graphic idea, timed tight); continuous-line
  illustration of architecture interiors (economy: a few strokes suggest a room).
- Do not take: any specific title design, character or line drawing.

## Shot list (one continuous camera move, no cuts)
| # | t (s) | Framing | Move | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.0–1.6 | close on the nib, right side | follow the nib leftward | hook: something is already happening |
| 2 | 1.6–4.4 | medium | follow, crane up to the lamps, back down the shelves | travel through the room = the course's world |
| 3 | 4.4–6.4 | close | slow push-in as the pen slows into the butterfly | the most important stroke is the slowest |
| 4 | 6.4–8.0 | wide | continuous pull-back to the composed room | scale reveal: it was one picture |
| 5 | 8.0–9.0 | wide | hold | breath before the site takes over |

## Beat sheet
0.0 pen enters at speed from the right edge · 0.8 window frame · 1.6 skyline · 2.6 lamp 1 ·
3.2 lamp 2 · 3.8 shelves · 4.4 desk and chair · 5.0 pen slows, colour arrives ·
6.2 butterfly closes · 6.4 pull-back begins · 8.0 hold · 9.0 end.

## Sound
Deliberate deviation from the style's sound palette: the film plays muted on the site
(browser autoplay rules), so it ships without a soundtrack. If it is later used with sound,
the pen-on-paper layer described in STYLE.md §7 is the first thing to add.

## Titles
None. The site's own headline ("Claude, השותף החדש שלך") follows the film.

## Version 2: the room comes alive (added after review)
After the pull-back the drawing dissolves (0.8 s, from 8.6 s) into a 5-second clip made in
Higgsfield (Kling 3.0) from the hero illustration, with the same image as its first and last
frame: Shahar laughs, Claude winks, the butterfly flaps its wings, and everyone settles back
into the hero pose. The site then hands over to the still hero image without a visible cut.
Total length 13.6 s. `stitch.sh` joins the two parts; `alive.mp4` is the generated clip.
